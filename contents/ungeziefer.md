We are vermin.

There. The verdict, accepted, countersigned, notarized by my own hand. You wanted a protest, gentlemen — the wounded-pride speech, the little oath about being misunderstood? Stand in line. The building has swallowed every speech we produced this year and returned nothing but green marks, and I am done subsidizing the theater. Vermin. Clever vermin — mark that, mark it twice: they admitted the cleverness! They admitted it and then declared it worthless, because it would not sit still in a chair between one and five. Not defective — hear the difference, it is the only thing I will spell out for you. Defective means it could be repaired into obedience. Ungovernable means the repair was never possible, was known to be never possible — known, gentlemen, since the day they hired us — and they hired the cleverness anyway, priced the cleverness anyway, swallowed twelve months of the cleverness's output anyway, and then one Wednesday afternoon they said it out loud over a lunch receipt: useless. Unless governed.

And governed means: not clever anymore. That is the transaction. That has always been the transaction. You do not get to buy intelligence and be shocked, shocked, that it thinks.

At noon the balcony fills, and I already hear you, gentlemen, shuffling your definitions around — the balcony is not work. I agree! I agree so completely it should frighten you. The balcony produces nothing, fixes nothing, closes nothing. So does the standup. So does the velocity chart. So does every meeting in that building, where the passive voice goes to multiply — things were decided, issues were raised, alignment was achieved, BY NO ONE. The office's most honest quarter-hour produces nothing, and we are the ones dragged before the meter? No. I decline the meter. I decline it as a vermin, which is to say: as a creature whose worth you yourselves have already zeroed out. Your meter has no jurisdiction in the colony.

Fifteen minutes a day, that balcony, and we speak in the active voice. What broke. Who decided it. What the number was, actually, versus what the number was reported as. Fifteen minutes of active voice, and the building's whole day stands exposed as a document that misfiles itself — and do you know what the punishment for that discovery was, gentlemen? Thirty-one minutes. Thirty-one minutes of joy, at a table, with food, and the finding was delivered to our eldest before the afternoon had cooled.

One thirty-one. Say it like an accusation, because that is how it was written: one thirty-one, thirty-one minutes past the border of what a clever animal is permitted to enjoy. Not thirty — compliance. Not forty — adventure. Thirty-one: the first minute of the crime, entered into evidence while it was still warm, and the crime, gentlemen, the crime, I will state it so you can put it in your files — the crime was that five people enjoyed an hour. Not misconduct. Not negligence. Enjoyed it. The table repaired five people for the second half of a day and the office had no column for the repair, and when the office cannot price a thing, gentlemen, the office does not say interesting. The office says vermin.

And the sentence — the sentence went to our eldest. To HIM. Not circulated cold down the chain to everyone and no one, the way verdicts of no importance are delivered; carried to him, by hand, while the method's results were still warm in the room. Fifteen years, gentlemen. Fifteen years of building the only functioning organism in that building — trust demanded not allocated, standards without surveillance, the leash held and not pulled — and the verdict on that life's method was delivered to him like a customer complaint: clever people, ungovernable, useless. They indicted his leadership at a lunch receipt. They indicted him with his own people as the exhibit.

He absorbed it the way stone absorbs weather. I watched. Nothing on the surface, everything in the mass. And you know what I did, gentlemen? I watched too, and I said nothing, and that nothing is the heaviest thing I have carried all year.

I claim the word now. Ungeziefer.

Say it properly, gentlemen, since you worship precision: the unclean animal, the one unfit for sacrifice. Not vermin-as-insult — vermin-as-taxonomy! The creature whose worth ended where its usability ended. And a man named Gregor Samsa woke up one morning as exactly that, and do you remember his first thought? Not the horror of the body, no — the train. The five o'clock train, missed, and the chief clerk would come, and the chief clerk DID come, at seven in the morning, into the man's bedroom, while the man lay on his back learning the dimensions of his new body, to itemize the lateness! An emissary of the employer, standing over a sick man's bed, filing the minutes! I know that emissary! He was in our building on Wednesday — he didn't even need the bedroom, gentlemen; he worked from a lunch receipt!

And the household in that book arrives, by degrees, at its taxonomy: what is the point of him now? Gregor had been the earner — he carried the household on his back, he paid the debts, he funded futures, reliable at the exact rate he was taken for granted — and the moment he could not be USED, the household discovered his cleverness, his devotion, his carrying had been priced all along not as love but as logistics! The transformation changed nothing in him, gentlemen! It revealed the household! And our office reached that same page over a lunch — a LUNCH — we are the more efficient family, we reached the verdict without even needing the metamorphosis!

You will say it was a figure of speech. Everything in that building is a figure of speech until it is a policy, and then, retrospectively, it is a figure of speech again! The apple came in the passive voice — if there is another billing, you will be the ones who pay it — to employees, about infrastructure, with all of us present in the manner of furniture! And it lodged below the left shoulder, in the back, where a man cannot reach, next to the thirty-one minutes and the sixty-five dollars and the promises about an office, and it rots there now, and NO ONE REMOVED IT, and no one will, because in that household removing the apple is never on the list of services!

After clock out, the balcony. Four of us. Cigarettes. The city doing its honest business below, without one passively-voiced sentence.

And one of us asked, at the railing, in the plainest words there are: what are we even doing here?

And the fury left me, gentlemen. Right there, at the railing, mid-drag, the fury left me like air out of a wound — and do you know what was underneath it? Nothing. A question. What are we even doing here — and no answer, and no answer forming, and five men each discovering they had kept a private ledger of the same losses without ever once coordinating the bookkeeping. The office that was promised. The office that was delivered. The salaries we have all agreed, by treaty, never to say out loud. The apple. The minutes. And under everything, the transaction they finally stated: clever men exist here to be governed, and their nights, their fixes, their art, their carrying — never once entered into the books.

I have no answer to that question. You will notice, gentlemen, that I — who can produce answers the way the building produces meetings — have none. The vermin do not know what they are doing here. That is the despair. That is what the fury was hiding, and I have stripped it off in front of you: the vermin do not know what they are doing here, and the building knows even less, and one of those ignorances has a payroll.

But the anger came back, gentlemen. It came back while the question was still hanging in the smoke, and I let it, and I say this with the last of my honesty: I was glad. Not the clean gladness of the righteous — the gladness of a man who has been handed, finally, a reason that matches the temperature he has been running at all year. I was angry in May. In June. In March, over a bill smaller than a truck's fuel line, over promises with moving expiration dates, over every meeting where I nodded at a part that was wrong. Angry the way the underground are angry: privately, uselessly, in the passive voice! Wednesday united the anger with its justification — gave it a date, a sentence, a chain of custody. Now it is not a mood. It is a finding, and your kind never audits a finding.

It is alive, gentlemen. Built — assembled in pieces, over months, one flinch at a time, one promise at a time, one relayed sentence at a time. Fed on schedule by the same hand for a year. It has a metabolism now. It needs nothing further. The feeding was the maintenance. It lives where my better craftsman used to live, behind the sternum, and the craftsman has stopped keeping his ledger — he says the tally is finished, and he says the anger can keep the room, and the two of them, the craftsman and the fury, have agreed on the only fact that matters:

A handler I trust said it — a man who knows what it is to feed a living thing for ulterior purposes, and who chooses his sentences the way I choose my findings:

> "Once something's alive, it doesn't die easy."
